Case study · Oct 2023 – Mar 2025

Powering an autonomous underwater vehicle

How our team designed, fabricated and tested the AUV's electrical system, and reached the SAUVC 2025 finals in Singapore.

My roleElectrical Head
TeamLed 15 juniors
OutcomeTop 6 / 45+ teams
AdvisorsDr. Gopinath, Dr. Sebastian

01 · The problem

A lot of electronics in a small, sealed hull

An AUV has to run a compute module, sensors and thrusters from a single battery, inside a waterproof enclosure that is hard to open once sealed. Anything that browns out, overheats or can't be shut down safely ends the run, or damages the vehicle.

The electrical system had to be compact, safe to operate in the pool, and dependable for long missions.

02 · What I built

Three pieces of hardware

Power distributionA distribution network with safety relays and a kill-switch interface, so the vehicle can be powered down instantly.
Pico sensor shieldA custom Raspberry Pi Pico shield that interfaces the sensors with the NVIDIA Jetson Xavier.
Interface PCBsCustom boards for sensor-to-Jetson communication, improving integration and deployment reliability.

Simplified architecture. Copper lines carry power; dashed lines carry data.

03 · Leading the team

Building people as well as boards

As Electrical Head (Apr 2024 – Apr 2025) I managed the vehicle's whole electrical system with a team of 15 juniors, and ran workshops on circuit design, PCB design and microcontrollers so the knowledge would stay in the club. I now mentor the next team preparing for TAC and RoboSub.
